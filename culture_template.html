Kumaon Cultural Atlas

Temples, forts, peaks, forests, birds, the region's registered GI products and visitor numbers, and places to stay along road circuits you can plan yourself, for Nainital, Almora, Bageshwar, Champawat, Pithoragarh and Udham Singh Nagar.
Move over the map for elevation and district. Select a marker for details.
Hold Ctrl (⌘ on Mac) and scroll to zoom, or use + and −

Plan a circuit

    Each stop is a night's base. Add any marker from the map with its Add to trip button. Drive times come from OpenStreetMap's road network at rough hill-road speeds; they leave out breaks, traffic and the monsoon landslides that close roads most years from July to September.

    Places to stay

    Sites

    From OpenStreetMap and Wikidata, merged where they name the same place. Ranked by how widely each is written about: the number of Wikipedia language editions, with an English article and a photo counting extra. Small village shrines are on the map but rarely make the top of this list.

    Birds

    Species and hotspots from eBird (Cornell Lab of Ornithology), counting full species only. Hotspots named for a private home are left off. The map's bird-record layer is GBIF's count of all bird occurrence records, which mostly come from eBird too: it shows where people watch birds as much as where birds are.

    Forest and land cover

    ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m, read here at about 100 m), share of each district's area. Forest on this map is WorldCover's tree cover; it doesn't separate oak, pine and sal, or natural forest from plantation. Protected-area outlines are OpenStreetMap's.

    GI products

    Visitors by destination

    Yearly visitor counts published by the Uttarakhand Tourism Development Board, 2021–2025. 2021 was a Covid year. Counts are the board's own, from hotel and site registers; they aren't comparable to census-style surveys.
